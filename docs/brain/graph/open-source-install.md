# Open sourcing brain ships the user's own workflow under Apache 2.0, with a NOTICE in his name

What ships is the user's own workflow: the open and closed agents, the gates, the way it talks. That is accepted; his YouTube videos explain why and how it works. The CPU rules of the user's own machine do not come with it; they stay in his own setup. The shipped settings do not default to auto mode. How a stranger installs it is in `install`.

The license is Apache 2.0, very permissive, with a NOTICE in his real name, Carlos Kelkboom, that travels with every copy and derivative, and the README invites people to say what they built with it.

When the source opens, its history goes with it as it is. The user does not mind that the history shows brain started as a private project and grew into something he wants to share.

## Why
The user wants to open source brain to help people and to start a community. The CPU rules guard one dying i9 and pin work to CPUs 16 to 31, which fails on a machine with fewer cores. The shipped settings once carried auto mode configured for the user's private CkLang repo, which is wrong for anyone else and gives away more permission than a stranger chose. Apache 2.0 over MIT because its NOTICE keeps his name with derivatives, the closest a permissive license comes to the mention he wants.
